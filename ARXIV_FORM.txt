TITLE
Context as a Cellular Automaton: A Survey of Local-Rule Memory and Context Management for LLM Agents

AUTHORS
Gong ZhengQin

ABSTRACT (arXiv limit 1920 chars; this one fits)
Long-horizon LLM agents must decide which fragments of an unbounded past enter a bounded context window, and which fragments of a new interaction to write back. We argue that most memory systems for such agents -- atomic-fact stores, extracted knowledge graphs, and entity-centric narrative profiles -- implement the same object: a discrete-time, local-rule dynamical process on a graph of memory units, i.e. a (graph) cellular automaton. A seven-component formalism (cells, neighbourhood, state, transition rule, schedule, readout, training signal) re-describes representative systems and exposes that the transition rule is almost always either a cosine threshold or a full LLM call, that cell state is usually binary, that fan-out is rarely controlled, and that per-hop evidence annotations in existing benchmarks are unused trajectory supervision. We survey candidate transition functions along a cost axis and argue that small non-generative decision models occupy the missing middle. Experiments on 300 multi-hop questions with per-hop labels (MemHop) test the central falsifier, a same-budget one-shot reranker: the released threshold rule of a current profile-memory system percolates to 96% of its store and a 9x readout, and a 22M-parameter cross-encoder applied once selects better cells (+0.15 chain recall, 95% CI [+0.11, +0.19]); the result survives removing the narrative layer, atomic cells, and distractors. The neighbourhood earns its place only when the rule reads the edge: gating on the seed's sentence about the candidate beats the one-shot ranker by +0.16 at forty cells (+0.22 on multi-hop questions). On the write path, the released rule leaves 61% of cells holding a changed fact untouched, while propagation along the neighbourhood restores consistency above 95% at a selectivity cost only a trained gate can pay. Locality is a cost where a global reader exists and the rule ignores the edge, an advantage where the rule reads the edge, and the only means of reach where no global reader exists.
